How to read the numbers

Five position lenses.

These are comparison guides, not Super64 player rankings. They use established college-football stat categories and tell fans what each group of numbers can—and cannot—show.

QB

Quarterbacks

Start with passing efficiency, completion rate, yards per attempt, touchdowns, interceptions, sacks, and rushing production. Volume without efficiency can hide trouble.

RB

Running backs

Use rushing yards per game, yards per carry, touchdowns, receiving work, and fumbles together. One long run should not define the whole profile.

WR

Receivers & tight ends

Receptions, receiving yards per game, yards per reception, and touchdowns show workload, explosiveness, and finishing value from different angles.

DEF

Defenders

Tackles, tackles for loss, sacks, interceptions, passes defended, and forced fumbles help separate steady involvement from game-changing disruption.

ST

Specialists

Field-goal percentage, punt average, kickoff returns, and punt returns matter most with attempt volume and field-position context beside them.

01

Production

What the player has actually done in the selected season.

02

Efficiency

How much value came from each attempt, touch, target, or snap.

03

Context

Opponent quality, role, availability, and game situation.

04

Trend

Whether the performance is strengthening, fading, or volatile.

Product design note: the lenses are Super64 editorial guidance. NCAA statistics supply the official-reference category set; CollegeFootballData supplies structured data when connected.
